## Terminology decisions

| Original wording | Working wording or rule | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Temperament and character | Affective temperament | Character was not separately measured |
| MPPS-MD | Turkish TEMPS-A, full English name on first use | Recognizable instrument name; confirm administered version |
| Depression / cyclothymia / anxiety as table headings | Depressive / cyclothymic / anxious temperament scores | These are scale dimensions, not diagnoses |
| More prevalent temperament | Higher mean temperament score | No dominant-temperament prevalence analysis was performed |
| Patients suffering from schizophrenia | Patients with schizophrenia | Precise, person-centered wording |
| Healthy people | Healthy controls, with screening method stated | Health status requires a recruitment definition |
| Non-offender outpatients | Psychiatric outpatients | Offending history was not verified in supplied data |
| Criminal patients / dangerous patients | Forensic psychiatric inpatients | Legal status does not establish individual dangerousness |
| Temperament triggers crime | Association with forensic inpatient group membership | Cross-sectional comparison cannot establish temporality or prediction |
| Small significant difference | Report estimate/test; describe magnitude separately | p-value does not establish a small effect |

Do not modernize historical diagnostic labels by guessing their codes. Confirm the diagnostic system and diagnoses first. Intellectual disability and cognitive impairment should remain distinct. Capacity for informed consent and ability to complete a questionnaire are different assessments.

## Section-specific audit

**Title/Abstract:** Cross-sectional design is stated. Abstract reports the actual temperament direction and confidence interval, not a causal mechanism. Its analysis wording remains conditional on the agreed hierarchy. Unsupported diagnosis percentages are excluded.

**Introduction:** Remove an unsubstantiated historical claim of improved adherence/rehabilitation explaining population crime trends. Define temperament and state the specific group-comparison question. Broad assertions about personality disorders or male risk distract from the measured construct unless directly supported.

**Methods:** Confirm recruitment, diagnostic ascertainment, clinical stability, control screening and matching. Describing groups as age/education matched is not justified by a nonsignificant age test; education distributions differ. State actual consent safeguards and instrument scoring. Replace the false all-normal-distributions statement.

**Results:** Use generated means/SDs and counts. Do not call all demographic differences patient-versus-control pairwise findings based only on omnibus chi-square tests. Do not infer prevalence, diagnosis proportions or income from unsupported labels. Present score differences with uncertainty.

**Discussion:** The original explanation of higher forensic depression was built on a reversed result and should be removed. Medication-induced symptoms are not evidence of depressive temperament causation. Discuss Dib and Çatlı without treating mixed results as confirmation of a single mechanism. Hospitalization, diagnosis, symptoms and social selection remain alternative explanations.

**Limitations:** Single center, male-only sample as reported by authors, modest unequal groups, convenience controls, unmeasured confounding, self-report, cross-sectional timing, multiplicity and unavailable item-level data. A general psychiatric inpatient comparator would help separate hospitalization from forensic status in future studies.

**Conclusion:** Higher mean irritable scores characterize the forensic inpatient group in this sample. No clinical risk-prediction recommendation is justified by these data.

### Data integrity rules

- Use only observed study data and explicitly attributed author confirmations. No artificial participants, invented measurements, guessed diagnosis labels, fabricated p-values or simulated observations presented as study data.
- Do not backfill uncollected symptom, medication, substance-use, matching or recruitment information. Mark it unavailable or pending confirmation.
- Preserve original files. Any verified correction must have a documented source and reproducible transformation; no silent recoding or deletion.
- Do not impute missing values in this revision without a separately justified, documented analysis decision; never present imputed values as observed.
- Exact/Monte Carlo inference or resampling, if methodologically justified, is computation on the observed data, not new participants. Label the procedure and retain its settings/seed.
- Do not choose covariates, subgroups or reporting methods because they produce significance. Report null and conflicting findings as well.
- Distinguish the original hypothesis from analyses added during revision. No retrospective claim of preregistration or prespecification.
- Author reported that item-level questionnaires exist but cannot be shared with an LLM. Verification of those records stays with the research team.
