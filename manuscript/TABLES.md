## Table 3 Affective temperament scores

| Dimension | Forensic mean ± SD | Outpatient mean ± SD | Control mean ± SD | F(2,112) | Overall p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Depressive | 10.09 ± 4.27 | 12.68 ± 4.29 | 3.79 ± 3.02 | 32.126 | <.001 |
| Cyclothymic | 11.27 ± 5.34 | 11.28 ± 4.95 | 4.71 ± 3.92 | 16.418 | <.001 |
| Hyperthymic | 12.52 ± 4.76 | 10.24 ± 5.40 | 10.96 ± 3.20 | 2.568 | .081 |
| Irritable | 11.58 ± 4.78 | 8.12 ± 4.00 | 2.29 ± 1.83 | 44.248 | <.001 |
| Anxious | 10.12 ± 4.83 | 12.32 ± 5.12 | 3.38 ± 3.90 | 24.966 | <.001 |

Forensic n = 66; outpatient n = 25; control n = 24. Groups were compared using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey tests. Comparisons between forensic inpatients and outpatients were significant for depressive (p = .020; SE of difference = 0.952) and irritable scores (p = .002; SE = 0.978). Outpatients had higher depressive scores, and forensic inpatients had higher irritable scores. The other three comparisons were not significant. Both patient groups scored higher than controls in depressive, cyclothymic, irritable and anxious temperament (each Tukey p < .001). No dominant-temperament thresholds were applied. SD, standard deviation; SE, standard error.
